Learn Stakeholder Management with Phoebe

Managing up and sideways: the map of who decides, what they fear, and how a data leader earns the room. Six sessions for heads of data, analytics managers and senior individual contributors who need executives and peers they do not manage to say yes. The org chart says who signs; these sessions are about who those people listen to first, what each of them is measured on, how to manage the boss you have, how to sequence the conversations before the meeting, and how to recover when you lose someone. One constructed running case, a logistics company's new head of data asking for one customer-data platform, and one bench: the real advice network of 21 managers, computed in your browser.

The six sessions 🧭 · from the first map of the room to the long game

The running case is Fernhollow Freight, a constructed mid-size logistics company. Sunniva Holm, its newly hired head of data, wants one customer-data platform approved: one shared customer record across sales, depots and billing. Five executives and one long-serving depot planning manager stand between her and a yes. Fernhollow and everyone in it are invented for teaching, and every page says so.

What is honest here 🧾

Influence advice travels with famous numbers, and several of the most quoted have no source behind them. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
Who is asked for advice, betweenness, and who can still reach whom when someone leavesReally computed in your browser from the advice and friendship ties of 21 managers (Krackhardt, 1987)
How many managers end up backing a proposalModelled by one threshold rule printed on the widget and labelled on every number; read the order of the strategies, not the exact counts
"70 percent of change programmes fail"No evidence found. A 2011 review traced five published sources for the figure and found no valid empirical basis for any of them
"People leave managers, not companies"A slogan. The checkable version is narrower: Gallup reports that in a 2015 study of 7,272 US adults, one in two had left a job to get away from a manager
The trust equationA practitioner model from a 2000 consulting book, useful as a checklist; no peer-reviewed test was found
Apology against denial after a trust violationAbstract read of a 2004 experiment: the better repair depends on whether the violation was about competence or integrity. Session 6 teaches the nuance
